## Patterns

1. **"Cheap because pre-adoption" under-counts a rename's *internal* blast radius.** D-021 and R-001 both priced the rename as cheap by measuring only user-facing breakage (npm/marketplace/muscle-memory). Actual cost inside the planning system: two re-supersessions of the migration policy (D-023→D-024→D-025), five spawned risks (R-018/R-020/R-021/R-022/R-023), and eight tasks (T-035–T-042). The team calibrates name-change cost as external churn and omits the immutable-record/anchor/cutover-sequencing cost.

2. **Cross-cutting policies are specified at their happy path and only corrected when a risk exposes the boundary case.** D-023 ("prose immutable") couldn't express dangling anchors → R-022 → D-024; D-024 ("repoint moved targets") couldn't express name-bearing anchors → R-023 → D-025. Same shape as D-017 ("engines advisory") → R-016 ("but 18/20 crash cryptically") → D-020. Three policy decisions, each revised by the first risk that hit its unhandled case.

3. **The record set trends toward after-the-fact tidiness, so learning hides.** Zero of 45 tasks dropped, and T-029 carried stale post-rename prose until manually caught. Mechanical gates deliberately skip `docs/` planning records, so stale *forward-looking* guidance is invisible to tooling. Absence of dropped tasks here is not evidence of clean planning — it is a sign that reversed direction is groomed into consistency rather than recorded as a scar.

4. **(Strength — preserve) Risk front-loading is genuinely working: high-impact problems are being retired at planning time.** R-016 gated before any user; R-018 reserved the name before publish; R-022/R-023 fixed before the sweep shipped dangling anchors; T-025 spiked the weak non-Claude targets before v1 commits to them. Every archived risk resolved same-day or next-day. The red-team→triage→decompose cadence is catching things pre-runtime — keep it.
