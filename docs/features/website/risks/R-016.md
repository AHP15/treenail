## R-016: With the per-client limiter removed and the global cap deferred, nothing rate-limits token-solved abuse of the metered providers

- **Severity**: high
- **Likelihood**: medium
- **Disposition**: mitigate
- **Date**: 2026-07-05
- **Date resolved**:
- **Description**: D-017 removes the per-client store entirely and defers even a coarse global endpoint cap until "observed abuse." But Turnstile is a bot *filter*, not a rate *limiter*: it raises the cost of automated submission, it does not bound volume. An attacker using a solver farm, a headless real browser, or cheap human labor can still obtain valid tokens and submit at will, and a single legitimate-looking loop can too. With no cap of any kind in the request path, that traffic flows straight to the metered transactional-email API and the hosted-list provider — running up per-message cost, flooding the owner's inbox, and polluting the newsletter list: the exact metered resources D-006/R-008 exist to protect. "Gated on observed abuse" means the first observation *is* the damage — the cost, the inbox flood, or the list pollution has already landed before any cap is added, and adding it then requires a code deploy under fire.
- **Disposition rationale**: D-017's choice of Turnstile is not wrong; the gap is that it deferred the *volume* ceiling entirely while Turnstile only addresses *botness*. The ceiling can be supplied without our own PII-keyed store — at the providers, which meter cost anyway — so this is a missing guard (mitigate), not a decision to reverse (fix). D-017 already contemplates a coarse global cap "later"; the mitigation is to make the ceiling exist *day-one at the providers* so "observed abuse" is bounded and cheap rather than unbounded and expensive. High severity because the blast radius is direct provider cost/inbox/list damage; medium likelihood because it requires deliberate abuse, but Turnstile does nothing to prevent it once decided upon.
- **Action**: Mitigate by (1) configuring hard provider-side ceilings as the real volume cap — a monthly send/spend cap and per-window rate cap on the transactional-email provider, and a signup rate cap on the hosted-list provider — so the worst case is bounded at the provider, inside D-006/D-017's no-own-store posture; (2) wiring volume alerting (provider dashboards or a submission-count metric) so abuse is *observed early*, well before the deferred trigger, turning "gated on observed abuse" into an actionable signal instead of a post-mortem; (3) pre-specifying the deferred escalation so it is turn-on-fast, not design-under-fire — a coarse global (non-per-client, no client identifier) endpoint counter with a fixed per-window ceiling, explicitly *not* a per-client PII-keyed store (that would reintroduce R-010/R-011). Ship (1) and (2) with the forms; keep (3) specified-but-dormant per D-017's deferral, triggered by the alerting in (2). Tracked by T-022. Reviewed 2026-07-07 against the mitigation-complete smell (T-022 done): kept **mitigate**, not accepted — the in-request volume cap (3) is dormant by design and the operative controls are provider-dashboard ceilings + live alerting, so material residual stands and this is a permanent standing operational concern, not a closed one.
