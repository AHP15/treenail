## R-006: The pipeline centerpiece hides its explanatory content behind hover, losing it on touch, keyboard, and assistive tech

- **Severity**: medium
- **Likelihood**: medium
- **Disposition**: mitigate
- **Date**: 2026-07-05
- **Date resolved**:
- **Description**: D-012 puts the substantive explanation — "what each skill reads and writes" — into hover/tap states on the animated pipeline, which is the one element D-012 says explains the product. Touch devices have no hover, and tap on a scroll-linked diagram competes with scrolling; keyboard and screen-reader users get no equivalent path to that content at all. `prefers-reduced-motion` is handled, but that only addresses motion, not the fact that the *information* lives in an interaction a large share of visitors (mobile is often the majority of landing traffic) and all AT users cannot reach. The wow-piece degrades to a decorative diagram whose meaning is unavailable to the people it most needs to convince.
- **Disposition rationale**: The animated centerpiece (D-012) stays; the defect is that its meaning is gated behind pointer-hover, which is an implementation constraint fixable with a content-availability requirement rather than a design reversal.
- **Action**: Mitigate by requiring the reads/writes content to have a pointer-independent path: render it as real, in-DOM, focusable content (not hover-only tooltips) so it is keyboard-reachable and exposed to screen readers; provide a tap interaction distinct from scroll on touch (or show the content inline on small viewports); and ensure the static/reduced-motion fallback already carries the full text. Add this to the a11y acceptance check for the pipeline component. Tracked by T-008.
